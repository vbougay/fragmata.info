The Larnaca plant stands south-west of Larnaca airport, next to the town's sewage treatment works. Larnaca Municipality challenged its siting inside the salt-lake protection zone, and the Supreme Court rejected the challenge in 2001. The plant mainly supplies Nicosia and Larnaca: its water joins treated dam water at Tersefanou and goes on to Nicosia by conveyor.

It was first built by Larnaca Water Partners, a joint venture of the Israeli firms IDE Technologies and Oceana, under a 10-year build-own-operate contract. It produced its first water on 19 May 2001, a month late. When that contract expired in July 2011, the plant passed to the state. A new contractor, MN Larnaca Desalination, owned by Israel's Mekorot and Cyprus's Netcom, rebuilt it and has run it since 4 July 2015, 379 days later than planned. The contract runs to June 2040 at a base price of €0.594 per m³.

The rebuild cut its energy use to about 3.2 kWh per m³, against about 4.5 at the other plants, and with the state as owner it is the cheapest of the five. It runs on grid electricity; the Water Development Department (WDD) has said it is trying to add solar panels. Rough seas stopped it in March 2026, and a strike was averted on 13 August 2026 by a three-year collective agreement.
